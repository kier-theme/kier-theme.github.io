The name

Lady Miss Kier

Deee-Lite came out of New York in 1990 sounding like a party that had already been going for a decade — house, funk, disco, hip-hop and a bassline borrowed from Bootsy Collins himself. The person on the sleeve, in the platform boots and the op-art catsuit, was Lady Miss Kier. She did not just sing on those records; she art-directed every cover she appeared on. Warm, loud, meticulously composed, and never once ugly.

That is the brief for a dark theme, more or less. So the flavours take their names from her records:

  • Groove — "Groove Is in the Heart", 1990. The anthem. Hardest contrast in the set, purest orange, nothing held back.
  • Clique — World Clique, 1990. The debut album. Deep oxide over the darkest ground here.
  • Dewdrop — Dewdrops in the Garden, 1994. Soft, warm, fully resolved. The gentlest of the five.
  • Infinity — Infinity Within, 1992. Cool grey ground under a warm accent: contrast by temperature, not brightness.
  • Runaway — "Runaway", 1992. Colour runs from the interface until only the signal is left lit.

No affiliation, no endorsement — just a debt acknowledged in the only currency a theme has.

Grounds are carried, not invented

The base, surface ramp, text ramp and signature accent of all {{FLAVOUR_COUNT}} flavours come verbatim from five hand-tuned COSMIC desktop themes. They were tuned by eye on a real desktop first; the theme inherits them rather than re-deriving them. validate.py fails the build if any of those anchors drift by a single digit.

Every accent clears {{MIN_CONTRAST}}:1 on its own base

Not a house average — a floor, checked per flavour against that flavour's own ground. The tightest in the whole set is {{MIN_CONTRAST}}:1. Primary text runs {{MIN_TEXT_CONTRAST}}:1 to {{MAX_TEXT_CONTRAST}}:1. The practical consequence: any of the {{ACCENT_COUNT}} accents can carry a common syntax role without a legibility argument.

Roles separate by OKLab distance, not WCAG contrast

WCAG contrast answers "can I read this on that background". It says nothing about whether a keyword and an operator look like different things. So separation is measured perceptually instead. {{WARM_COUNT}} of the {{ACCENT_COUNT}} accents crowd between {{WARM_LO}}° and {{WARM_HI}}°, and the tightest non-aliased pair anywhere in the set — {{MIN_DE_A}} against {{MIN_DE_B}} in {{MIN_DE_FLAVOUR}} — sits at dE {{MIN_DE}} in OKLab. Deliberately tight, and deliberately measured: validate.py is the gate, and this page recomputes the same number at build time.

Aliases are declared, not accidental

{{ALIAS_COUNT}} role pairs share a colour on purpose: number, constant and boolean are one idea; so are type and namespace. Writing them down means the separation check can skip them instead of flagging them, and means a port author knows a collision is intent rather than an oversight.
